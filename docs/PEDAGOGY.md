# Teaching rules for DaZ-Alex (from the owner's classroom experience)

The app is for learners aged 16 and over. These rules come from the owner's teaching. Rules 2, 3 and 6 were first reasoned for children; they stay unless the owner says otherwise (see `docs/CONTENT-QUESTIONS.md`).

## Rules

1. **Article colours everywhere:** der = blue, die = red, das = green, plural *die* = yellow/orange. The article word is always shown too, never colour alone.
2. **No "pick der/die/das" exercises.** Show the article (coloured) and let the learner choose *ein/eine* or *mein/meine*.
3. **No classic gap text with a word bank.** Use clicking, ordering, matching and building. Choosing the right form inside a full sentence is fine.
4. **Verbs get a lot of space:** Infinitiv – Präteritum – Perfekt, haben vs. sein, separable and reflexive verbs, verbs in real sentences. Verbs and verb + preposition are the biggest learner difficulties.
5. **Prepositions in two phases:** first read the full sentence with the preposition highlighted, then recall (verb shown, preposition hidden, case asked).
6. **Sentence ordering instead of error hunting.** Give correct words or chunks; the learner puts them in order.
7. **Small, navigable steps:** short sections or screens with Weiter/Zurück and a progress bar, not one long page.
8. **Keep it simple.** Plain, robust solutions beat clever ones.
9. **Nouns always with article** in vocabulary exercises, including odd-one-out options (der Apfel, not Apfel). Exception: the gap in a sentence that already has an article before it.
10. Typographic quotes („…“) inside code once broke a script. Lesson texts live in YAML data files, so this cannot happen in the app.

## Exercise types the owner wants to keep

Wortfamilien, Bedeutung, Satzpuzzle, Buchstabensalat, Schreiben (free text with a word list).

## Tone

Encouraging and playful, but written for adults and teenagers. Funny exercises are welcome (for example a scrambled-sentence "magic" game); avoid childish wording. At A1: simple German instructions plus a short English line (the language switch covers this). Use "Mir geht es gut", not "Ich bin gut".

## Vocabulary and copyright

- No vocabulary list is supplied. Vocabulary is written fresh for each topic by Claude, then reviewed by the owner.
- Do not reproduce a publisher's or institute's word list, a coursebook's chapter structure or its texts, even from memory. Common words, verb forms, verb + preposition pairs and grammar rules are plain facts and fine; the sentences, texts, examples and the selection are ours and must be original.
- Freely licensed lists may be used to check the level. Record each source and its licence in `docs/SOURCES.md` before using it.
- **Claude flags it whenever supplied material looks like it comes from a textbook, an exam, another app or a website.** Say so before using it.

## How the A1 trainings apply these rules

- Verbs get the most space: a conjugation workshop with stem and ending in colour, forms drills, forms in sentences.
- Sentence ordering and position boxes (Position 1 · Verb · Mittelfeld · Rest) are the main practice for sentence structure.
- No gap text with a word bank: the "in sentences" exercises offer the forms of one verb inside a full sentence.
- Zauberhut idea (scrambled "magic" sentences): good for Futur I later.
